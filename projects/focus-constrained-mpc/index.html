Focus-Constrained Visual Predictive Control for Robotic Micro-Hole Localization

This project studies visual alignment for robotic microscopic measurement of micro-holes on curved surfaces, where a small field of view and shallow depth of field make nominal robot positioning insufficient.

I developed a robot-assisted measurement framework that combines coarse positioning with local visual feedback to bring a target into view and maintain usable focus for subsequent measurement.

InstitutionHuazhong University of Science and Technology
FocusRobotic perception · Microscopic alignment · Visual control
StatusOngoing research documentation and evaluation
01

Overview

Geometric priors can guide a robot toward a nominal hole location. Manufacturing variation and accumulated positioning errors can nevertheless leave the actual opening off-center, outside the microscopic view, or out of focus.

The research connects robot positioning with image-based local alignment. Its objective is to establish a clear, centered microscopic view that supports downstream image acquisition and micro-hole measurement.

02

Coordinated Motion

The microscope is mounted on a linear stage attached to the robot flange. The motion chain connects the robot base {B}, flange {E}, stage {M}, and camera {C}: robot motion positions the assembly, the flange-to-stage attachment is rigid, and the stage translates the camera along its travel direction.

Because the camera is offset from the flange, a change in robot orientation can also shift the camera position. Camera motion therefore depends on both the robot and the stage. With the stage approximately aligned to the optical axis, its fine travel supports focus adjustment while the robot provides local target positioning.

03

Method Overview

Lateral alignment and axial focus

A local search frame is defined near the nominal measurement pose. Two directions perpendicular to the optical axis describe lateral search and centering; the axial direction describes focus adjustment. These directions follow the microscope orientation rather than the global robot axes.

The robot primarily corrects lateral target position, while the linear stage provides fine focus adjustment. On a curved surface, lateral motion can change the required working distance, so focus must be monitored and compensated during alignment.

Image feedback closes the loop

Image sharpness indicates whether the view is usable; once the opening is visible, its displacement from the image center guides alignment. Predictive control coordinates small adjustments while considering focus, actuator limits, and clearance from the workpiece.
