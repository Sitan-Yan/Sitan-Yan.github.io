Robotic Trajectory Planning for Aircraft CFRP Horizontal Stabilizer Rib Machining

This project combines smooth trajectory planning, stiffness-aware robot posture selection, and cutting-parameter optimization for machining carbon-fiber-reinforced polymer (CFRP) rib features.

I developed robot models and optimization methods, then evaluated machining quality through CFRP milling experiments, surface-roughness measurements, and scanned contour analysis.

InstitutionHuazhong University of Science and Technology
AdvisorsProf. Wenlong Li · Dr. Wei Xu
PlatformABB IRB 6660-205/1.9 · CFRP milling
01

Overview

Robots offer a flexible workspace for machining large composite components, but pose-dependent stiffness and changes in cutting engagement can degrade edge quality. The objective was to improve motion efficiency and machining quality for features representative of aircraft horizontal stabilizer ribs.

The work connects kinematic and stiffness modeling with trajectory optimization, tool-axis posture selection, and physical machining trials. Experiments used CFRP plates and machined slots; they were not a full-aircraft-component production qualification.

02

Core Approach

The planning framework addresses both how the robot moves and which posture it uses to resist machining loads.

1

Kinematics & stiffness

Built a modified-DH model and derived the Jacobian. Related joint stiffness to Cartesian stiffness, then used stiffness and force ellipsoids to evaluate pose-dependent performance.

2

Smooth trajectory planning

Used piecewise fifth-order polynomial interpolation and an improved particle swarm optimizer to minimize trajectory duration under joint-motion and dexterity constraints.

3

Posture selection

Evaluated machining postures using stiffness and dexterity criteria, exploiting rotation about the tool axis while respecting robot joint limits.

03

Experimental Validation

A dust-extraction attachment was designed and integrated with the robotic machining platform. An L16 orthogonal experiment examined feed rate, milling depth, and spindle speed; surface roughness was measured using a TR200 instrument.

A follow-up trial at the selected settings—60 mm/min feed, 2 mm milling depth, and 6,000 rpm spindle speed—produced a measured Ra of 3.689 μm. Larger-plate tests used 500 × 300 × 3 mm CFRP specimens with 40 × 20 mm slots to compare robot postures and continuous versus separate-edge machining paths.

Scanned point clouds were also used to assess machined contours and corner transitions. The platform photograph above shows the large-plate experiment with the dust-extraction attachment removed for visibility.

04

Selected Results

  • 23 s → approximately 12.36 s planned trajectory duration. The optimized segment times improved on the initial time allocation in the trajectory-planning example.
  • 13.6% lower average edge-wise maximum Ra after posture optimization. Each edge was measured three times, with its maximum used for comparison.
  • 8.0% lower maximum Ra on edge II using separate-edge paths. This comparison kept the optimized posture and cutting settings unchanged.
  • 0.442 mm average contour error across the large-plate measurements, providing a geometric assessment alongside roughness.
